## A1. Kapitan Bomba — production, distribution, characters, humor, transferability

### Takeaway
Kapitan Bomba (2007–2013, 164 eps) proved that ultra-cheap, extremely vulgar sci-fi parody made by essentially one author-voice (Bartosz Walaszek) can create a national meme language ("walaszkizmy"). Crucially for the creator: the first episodes were only tens of seconds long, i.e. the show was born in a "short-form" format. Its humor is built on Polish profanity and Polish social types, so the jokes themselves do not travel; the mechanics (cheap look, one-voice cast, genre parody, gibberish-speaking villain, quotable lines) do.

### Cited Findings
- Polish adult animated comedy, 2007–2013, satirizing Polish politics and social life in a fictional sci-fi setting; 9 seasons, 164 episodes; creator Bartosz Walaszek, who also voiced most characters — [pl.wikipedia: Kapitan Bomba](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapitan_Bomba)
- Origin: a protest. In 2007 KRRiT chair Elżbieta Kruk ordered GIT's cartoons moved from 15:00 to a later slot (initially 22:00); Walaszek reportedly set out to make a show too controversial for TV — [pl.wikipedia](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapitan_Bomba); same story in [SilesiON.pl](https://silesion.pl/kapitan-bomba-kultowa-animacja-ktora-stworzyla-wlasny-jezyk-internetu/)
- **Format: early episodes lasted only tens of seconds; length grew as the project developed and storylines became continuous** — [pl.wikipedia](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapitan_Bomba); [SilesiON.pl](https://silesion.pl/kapitan-bomba-kultowa-animacja-ktora-stworzyla-wlasny-jezyk-internetu/)
- Distribution: debuted on music channel 4fun.tv in the block "Kartony"; production ended in 2013 after a conflict between Walaszek and 4fun.tv; episodes later on YouTube — [pl.wikipedia](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapitan_Bomba)
- Rights: in January 2020 CDA S.A. acquired the rights (incl. the 164 episodes and two feature films, plus "Laserowy gniew dzidy" and "Kaliber 200 Volt") — [pl.wikipedia](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapitan_Bomba); production company history: GIT Produkcja, later SPInka Film Studio — [TVP Info](https://www.tvp.info/94253053/kapitan-bomba-i-wielki-powrot-kim-jest-bartosz-walaszek)
- Films: "Kapitan Bomba: Kutapokalipsa" (23 March 2012) and "Kapitan Bomba: Zemsta Faraona" (5 Oct 2012); spin-offs "Laserowy Gniew Dzidy", "Galaktyczne Lektury" — [pl.wikipedia](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapitan_Bomba)
- Reach: as of Feb 2023, YouTube episodes had from hundreds of thousands to several million views; "R.K.S. CHUWDU" had 3.4M; Facebook page ~190k followers (Jan 2020); the fictional football club RKS Chuwdu attracted real fans — [pl.wikipedia](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapitan_Bomba)
- Characters (archetypes): Tytus Bomba — Galactic Fleet soldier, militarist, hates aliens, workaholic, failure with women (hero undercut by flaws); Sebastian "Sebek" Bąk — weak, talkative soldier (photography, Counter-Strike); Janusz Sram — quiet soldier (fishing, cooking); J.J. Torpeda — sensible rival, later Admiral; **Sułtan Kosmitów ("Otyły Pan") — main villain who speaks a stylized Italian-like gibberish**; Bogdan "Lufa" Bomba — abusive alcoholic general father; Bogusław Łęcina — builder parody; Mariku1234 — parody of a real internet hater of Walaszek — [pl.wikipedia](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapitan_Bomba)
- Catchphrases that entered everyday Polish speech: "wóda, dziwki i lasery"; "Czujesz ten zapach? Tak pachnie sto złotych"; sayings from Walaszek's shows are collectively called "walaszkizmy"; fan chant "Walaszek grubasie, kiedy Bomba?" became an internet phrase during the hiatus and was remixed many times — [SilesiON.pl](https://silesion.pl/kapitan-bomba-kultowa-animacja-ktora-stworzyla-wlasny-jezyk-internetu/); [pl.wikipedia](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapitan_Bomba)
- Humor type: described as a key Polish example of shock comedy — extremely vulgar or absurd content meant to shock — [pl.wikipedia](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapitan_Bomba); [SilesiON.pl](https://silesion.pl/kapitan-bomba-kultowa-animacja-ktora-stworzyla-wlasny-jezyk-internetu/)
- Running gag of the language itself: per a trope wiki (seen only as a search snippet, page returned 403), Bomba punishes his soldiers for politeness and forces them to swear — the inverted-politeness gag is a core joke engine — [All The Tropes: Kapitan Bomba](https://allthetropes.org/wiki/Kapitan_Bomba) (unverified, snippet only)
- 2026 revival flop: a new episode "Wielki powrót" premiered on YouTube on 12 July 2026 (first in ~13 years); YouTube description credits Walaszek as writer/director but Walaszek/SPInka had publicly denied involvement; fans received it lukewarmly — criticised as chaotic, jumping between plotlines, "cheap and crude", relying again on sexual/genital jokes — [CD-Action, 13 Jul 2026](https://cdaction.pl/kultura/nowy-kapitan-bomba-juz-do-obejrzenia-wielki-powrot-zostal-jednak-chlodno-przyjety-przez-fanow/); [gram.pl (Walaszek's studio distances itself)](https://www.gram.pl/news/kapitan-bomba-bez-bartosza-walaszka-tworca-animacji-odcina-sie-od-nowych-odcinkow); [Filmweb](https://www.filmweb.pl/news/%22Kapitan+Bomba%22:+nowy+odcinek+na+YouTube.+Kiedy+premiera-167516)

### Inferences
- Format precedent: Bomba's first episodes were sub-minute — a direct Polish precedent that a series can start as micro-episodes and still become cult; the 60–120 s format is not a limitation.
- What spread was **short, rhythmic, list-like or sensory lines** ("wóda, dziwki i lasery" = 3-item list; "tak pachnie sto złotych" = absurd sensory claim). Lists-of-three and absurd-confident statements are structures that can be re-created in English.
- The **gibberish-speaking villain** (Sułtan's pseudo-Italian) is the most globally transferable device in the show: it is funny through sound and intonation, needs no translation, and is cheap to produce with TTS/voice design.
- The 2026 revival shows that vulgarity alone, without strong character continuity and a clear authorial voice, reads as "cheap and crude" — vulgarity is seasoning, not the dish.
- Too local: Polish profanity morphology, Polish slang/neologisms, references to Polish football clubs, politicians, and TV; the "kiedy Bomba?" meme depends on fandom history.

### Gaps
- No source found for Kapitan Bomba's budget, cost per episode, team size, or animation software (widely assumed to be simple 2D/Flash-style cut-out, but not confirmed by any source found).
- No view count found for the July 2026 "Wielki powrót" episode.
- How the 2012 feature films were distributed (cinema vs. online/DVD) and their audience numbers not found.
- No evidence of any foreign-language version of Kapitan Bomba.

## A2. Blok Ekipa (Hoodies Squad) — production, distribution, characters, humor

### Takeaway
Blok Ekipa is the clearest benchmark for a lean, author-driven adult web series: ~1 week per episode, made essentially by two brothers (Bartosz writes/voices/edits, Łukasz animates), YouTube-first since 2013, 300+ episodes, later licensed to Comedy Central Polska (2015–2024), and even partially dubbed into Russian. Its humor is hyper-local (Warsaw "dresy", slang, Polish celebrities/politicians), but its archetype — three dumb, loyal, lazy losers from the block — is universal.

### Cited Findings
- Note on the brief: no evidence was found of a 2014 Blok Ekipa cinema film; it is a web series. A 2015 article mentions only a *planned* crowdfunding campaign for a feature-length animated film — [naTemat, 3 Mar 2015](https://natemat.pl/135271,blok-ekipa-to-animowany-serial-na-youtube-o-typowych-trzech-dresach-sa-chamscy-maja-czarny-humor-i-ogladaja-ich-miliony); the [en.wikipedia "Hoodies Squad"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoodies_Squad) and [pl.wikipedia](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blok_Ekipa) pages list no film
- Premiered 10 June 2013 on Walaszek's YouTube channel SPInka Film Studio; 303 episodes + 2 specials, 14 seasons (as of 7 Jan 2025); running time 4–23 min; several episodes dubbed into Russian as "Na Rayonye" — [en.wikipedia: Hoodies Squad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoodies_Squad)
- TV licensing: broadcast on Comedy Central Polska from 4 Oct 2015 to 1 Nov 2024 — [pl.wikipedia: Blok Ekipa](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blok_Ekipa)
- Voices: nearly all characters including the protagonists are voiced by Walaszek; several female roles by Katarzyna Kralewska-Walaszek — [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoodies_Squad)
- **Production process (2015):** Walaszek outlines the plot in bullet points → writes and records dialogue (a few hours) → animation ~4 days → editing, music, SFX a few more hours → ~1 week per episode, often done at the last minute; team: Bartosz (concept, scripts, editing, voices) + brother Łukasz (animator); premieres on Tuesdays (fixed weekly slot) — [naTemat, 3 Mar 2015](https://natemat.pl/135271,blok-ekipa-to-animowany-serial-na-youtube-o-typowych-trzech-dresach-sa-chamscy-maja-czarny-humor-i-ogladaja-ich-miliony)
- Source of jokes, in Walaszek's words: "Obserwuję co się dzieje dookoła" — he watches what happens around him, listens to how people talk, watches YouTube clips, then adds his twist; studio motto "daj się ponieść absurdowi" — [naTemat](https://natemat.pl/135271,blok-ekipa-to-animowany-serial-na-youtube-o-typowych-trzech-dresach-sa-chamscy-maja-czarny-humor-i-ogladaja-ich-miliony)
- Reach 2015: channel passed 200k subscribers; each new episode got hundreds of thousands of views; Spejson's Valentine vlog spin-off >1.5M views; merch (T-shirts) sold well — [naTemat](https://natemat.pl/135271,blok-ekipa-to-animowany-serial-na-youtube-o-typowych-trzech-dresach-sa-chamscy-maja-czarny-humor-i-ogladaja-ich-miliony)
- Reach 2026 (aggregator, approximate): SPInka Film Studio channel ~2.6M subscribers, ~2B views, 747 videos (data 19 May 2026); episode "BLOK EKIPA (300)" (early April 2026) ~2.09M views; most other 2026 episodes tens to hundreds of thousands — [speakrj audit](https://www.speakrj.com/audit/report/UCxJDH_2HXzwUtT62HgWJqCg/youtube/media-stats/) (third-party, treat as approximate)
- Characters/archetypes: three lazy "dresiarze" (20–30 y.o., working-class, Legia fans) from Warsaw's Grochów — Spejson (strongest, dumbest; MMA, vlogs), Wojtas (car nut: tuned Polonez "Szerszeń", then BMW E36 "Bawara"), Walo (the "inventive" one, works at a garage); antagonists Rudy (snitch) and Cieślak (rival); Cjalis — disco-polo singer, the show's counterpart of Walaszek — [pl.wikipedia](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blok_Ekipa); [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoodies_Squad)
- Language as comedy: blokers' slang with profanity replacing neutral words (in ep. 28 a swear word replaces "powiedzieć"); neologisms "monopolik" (liquor store), "poka", "setunia"; deliberately mangled foreign words and grammar; "swoi vs obcy" worldview — [pl.wikipedia](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blok_Ekipa)
- Topicality: comments on current events (e.g., Łazienkowski Bridge fire), caricatures of media/internet celebrities and politicians (Kwaśniewski, Misiewicz, Macierewicz, Trzaskowski) — [pl.wikipedia](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blok_Ekipa)

### Inferences
- Benchmark for the creator: two people produced 4–23 min episodes weekly; with AI animation and ElevenLabs, a solo creator should be able to produce 60–120 s episodes at 2–5 per week. The fixed weekly slot (Tuesdays) built a habit.
- The "trio of idiots" (strong-dumb / obsessive / "smart" one) is a universal sitcom archetype (cf. Three Stooges, Beavis & Butt-Head); the specific Polish dres costume and slang are not. For global use: keep the dynamic, swap the costume to something recognizable worldwide (e.g., gym bros, crypto bros, office slackers).
- Comedy-through-slang is a strength domestically and the main barrier abroad; the Russian "Na Rayonye" dub shows the format can be localized into culturally close markets where an equivalent subculture exists (gopniki) — localization works when the target culture has its own version of the archetype.
- Merch success suggests strong character identity monetizes beyond views.

### Gaps
- No budget, revenue, or cost-per-episode figures found.
- Animation software/technique not documented in sources found.
- No data on the reach of the Russian dub.

## A3. Jakub Wędrowycz — does an animation exist, and why the character works

### Takeaway
No official animated (or live-action) adaptation of Jakub Wędrowycz was found as of Nov 2026; the character lives in books (11 collections), comics, a card game, audiobooks and a real-world fan festival. His appeal is a template the creator can use: a filthy, lawless, elderly antihero who is nevertheless **competent** and wins — a lovable embodiment of national vices — delivered in short standalone stories.

### Cited Findings
- Character: antihero — elderly alcoholic, moonshiner, poacher and self-taught village exorcist from Stary Majdan (gmina Wojsławice, Lublin region); Ukrainian/Cossack roots, only 3 classes of primary school; openly defies government and authority; his name is a pun on a literal Polish translation of "Johnnie Walker" — [en.wikipedia: Jakub Wędrowycz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakub_W%C4%99drowycz)
- Debut: story "Hiena", Fenix magazine, Feb 1996; first book "Kroniki Jakuba Wędrowycza" (2001); 11 collections as of 2025 (latest 2025, "Wojsławicka masakra kosą…") — [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakub_W%C4%99drowycz); [naszeszlaki.pl](https://www.naszeszlaki.pl/archives/94445)
- Adaptations that exist: comics "Dobić dziada" (2011) and "Zabójca" (2014), drawn by Andrzej Łaski; collectible card game (2011); a new comic "Klątwa C2H5OH" (2026, Fabryka Słów); audio versions — [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakub_W%C4%99drowycz); [naszeszlaki.pl](https://www.naszeszlaki.pl/archives/94445); [Audioteka cycle](https://audioteka.com/pl/cykl/jakub-wedrowycz/)
- Screen adaptation status: "no officially confirmed cinema or series adaptation" — [naszeszlaki.pl](https://www.naszeszlaki.pl/archives/94445). A Facebook page "Kroniki Jakuba Wędrowycza – serial" exists, describing a series based on the stories, but gives no studio, format (animated or not), broadcaster or date; unverified — [Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/p/Kroniki-Jakuba-W%C4%99drowycza-serial-61570453082826/)
- Popularity markers: fan festival "Dni Jakuba Wędrowycza" in Wojsławice since 2006; 3 m wooden statue unveiled 2013; PTTK tourist badge (2016); Jacek Dukaj called him one of the most colorful, original, memorable characters of Polish SF/F, comparable to Geralt; translated into Czech (described as very popular there) and partly Russian — [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakub_W%C4%99drowycz)
- Why readers like him: stories are short, light and standalone; the hero is colorful and instantly recognizable; fantasy + Polish province + satire of Polish vices; unpredictable but effective methods; humor is grotesque, blunt, black, absurd, a parody of horror/fantasy/SF; some scenes cross the line of good taste — [naszeszlaki.pl](https://www.naszeszlaki.pl/archives/94445)
- He is seen in Poland as a mix of popular negative stereotypes and vices of Poles that still evokes sympathy — [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakub_W%C4%99drowycz)

### Inferences
- Transferable formula: "gross old outlaw who is secretly the only competent person in the room" + genre parody (horror/monsters) + standalone episodes. This archetype exists globally (cf. Rick Sanchez in Rick and Morty — inference, not sourced here), so it is a safe international character engine.
- Monster/supernatural parody is visually legible without dialogue (a demon, a zombie, an exorcism with a bottle of moonshine) → good for nonverbal 60–120 s gags.
- Too local: the Johnnie Walker name pun, Lublin countryside dialect, Polish-Ukrainian borderland history, Polish bureaucracy specifics.
- The Czech success suggests culturally adjacent markets "get" the rural-boozer antihero; for global appeal, the vices should be universal (drinking, laziness, greed, defiance of authority), not tied to a national stereotype.
- Licensing note (inference): Wędrowycz is a protected Fabryka Słów/Pilipiuk IP; the creator should borrow the archetype, not the character.

### Gaps
- No evidence found of any professional or fan animated short about Wędrowycz; searches in Polish returned nothing (could be indexing gaps).
- No sales/circulation figures for the book series found.

## A4. South Park — production model, reach, deals, characters, humor (2025–2026 focus)

### Takeaway
South Park's model is "speed + topicality + crude simplicity": a deliberately primitive cut-out look produced in Maya on a ~6-day cycle, with two creators voicing most male characters. In 2025 it was revalued at ~$1.5B (Paramount+, 5 years, 50 episodes) and Season 27's Trump satire produced its biggest premiere since 1999 (5.9M viewers in 3 days) — but by the Season 29 premiere (Sept 2026) total viewers fell to 4.2M (-29% vs S27), showing that political-shock spikes fade. Its globally portable assets are visual/running gags (Kenny's death), archetypes, and the simple look; US politics is not.

### Cited Findings
**Production**
- Pilot "Cartman Gets an Anal Probe" was made with real cut-out (construction paper) animation and took ~3 months; later episodes made in computer animation, mainly Autodesk Maya, designed to imitate the cut-out look — [en.wikipedia: South Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Park)
- Episodes typically completed in one week, some in 3–4 days; scripts are not written before the season starts; finished episode delivered to Comedy Central on Wednesday, sometimes hours before the 10 PM airtime — [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Park); six-days-per-episode working rhythm — [Looper](https://looper.com/1092821/south-parks-breakneck-production-is-managed-down-to-the-minute)
- Team/budget: staff ~70; each episode originally cost $250,000 — [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Park); the official site says most animation is done in Maya by a team of about 10 animators (undated, may be out of date) — [South Park Studios FAQ](https://southpark.cc.com/news/78zs1w/fan-question-do-you-hand-draw-every-scene)
- Topical speed example: "Quintuplets 2000" referenced a raid that happened four days before airing — [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Park)

**Voices and characters**
- Trey Parker and Matt Stone voice most male characters — [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Park)
- Archetypes: Stan (average boy, Parker's stand-in), Kyle (Jewish, moral voice, Stone's stand-in), Cartman (amoral, increasingly psychopathic antagonist), Kenny (poor kid, parka muffles his speech; died in nearly every episode of seasons 1–5) — [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Park)
- "Oh my God, they killed Kenny!" / "You bastards!" — call-and-response trademark gag used in many early episodes, originating in the short "The Spirit of Christmas: Jesus vs. Frosty" — [en.wikipedia: Kenny McCormick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenny_McCormick); Cracked lists it with "Screw you guys, I'm going home" as the show's two biggest lines, and notes Cartman's "Respect My Authoritah!" "lends itself perfectly to image macros" — [Cracked: 25 Greatest South Park Memes](https://cracked.com/article_40502_the-25-greatest-south-park-memes.html)
- Humor: profanity, dark and surreal humor, satire of everything; self-described "equal opportunity offender"; creators say it is about "kids being kids" — [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Park)

**Distribution and deals**
- Premiered on Comedy Central 13 Aug 1997; HBO Max streaming rights from 24 June 2020 (reported $500–550M) — [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Park)
- 21–23 July 2025: new 5-year deal; South Park Digital Studios licensed the show exclusively to Paramount+ (global streaming home), reported ~$1.5B; covers 50 new episodes over five seasons plus all 26 previous seasons; new episodes continue on Comedy Central — [Variety](https://variety.com/2025/tv/news/south-park-paramount-plus-billion-dollar-deal-1236466524/); [Deadline](https://deadline.com/2025/07/south-park-creaors-overall-deal-paramount-streaming-1236466139/); [Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/tv/tv-news/south-park-paramount-deal-5-years-1236324420/); Forbes reported the creators became billionaires (search snippet framed it as ~$250M/year to Parker & Stone through 2030 — conflicts with simple $1.5B/5 = $300M/yr; treat as uncertain) — [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/mattcraig/2025/07/25/south-parks-creators-are-now-billionaires/)
- S27 premiere was delayed from July 9 to July 23, 2025 amid the contract dispute — [Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/tv/tv-news/south-park-paramount-deal-5-years-1236324420/) (via search summary)

**Season 27 (2025) virality**
- Premiere "Sermon on the Mount" (23 July 2025): Trump threatens to sue the town, is shown in bed with Satan and confronting an artist over the size of his anatomy; Jesus returns "because of a lawsuit and an agreement with Paramount" (jab at Paramount's $16M "60 Minutes" settlement) — [Straight Arrow News](https://san.com/cc/south-park-premiere-mocking-trump-breaks-26-year-viewership-record/)
- 5.9M viewers across Comedy Central + Paramount+ in 3 days; biggest season-opener audience in ~25–26 years; 0.996 rating among adults 18–49 — [Straight Arrow News](https://san.com/cc/south-park-premiere-mocking-trump-breaks-26-year-viewership-record/); Paramount also said it was the show's biggest episode ever across social channels, cable share 9.2 (via search summary of TheWrap) — [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/?p=7809937)
- White House spokesperson said the show "hasn't been relevant for over 20 years"; Parker replied at Comic-Con, deadpan: "We're terribly sorry" — [Straight Arrow News](https://san.com/cc/south-park-premiere-mocking-trump-breaks-26-year-viewership-record/)
- A later S27 episode skewering Kristi Noem ("ICE Barbie") was reported as breaking another record (headline only, not verified) — [Daily Beast](https://www.thedailybeast.com/south-park-breaks-new-record-after-brutal-ice-barbie-kristi-noem-episode)

**Seasons 28–29 (decay of the spike)**
- Season 29 premiere (article 25 Sept 2026): 4.2M viewers across 3 days (US, multiplatform); S28 premiere was 4.3M; S27 premiere 5.9M; 18–49 rating up 33% vs S28 (0.73 → 0.97); 11M social views and 1M engagements in first 3 days; 63k online mentions (+40% vs S28); S29 episodes biweekly (Sept 30, Oct 14, Oct 28, Nov 11, Nov 25); streams on Paramount+ same day in UK, next day in US/Canada/Australia, Saturdays in all other markets — [TheWrap](https://thewrap.com/creative-content/tv-shows/south-park-season-29-premiere-ratings)
- TheWrap headline frames S29 premiere as "down 29% from last year" — [TheWrap](https://thewrap.com/creative-content/tv-shows/south-park-season-29-premiere-ratings)

**Short-form presence**
- Official TikTok @southpark listed with ~7.7M followers (snapshot updated 19 Oct 2026; TikTok discover page, low reliability), posting short scene clips and promos; one Kyle/Cartman clip ~3.1M likes — [TikTok discover](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/south-park-official-tiktok); the official X account's behind-the-scenes post (29 July 2025) exceeded 13M views — [Straight Arrow News](https://san.com/cc/south-park-premiere-mocking-trump-breaks-26-year-viewership-record/)

### Inferences
- Implied license value ≈ $30M per new episode ($1.5B / 50 eps, ignoring library value) vs. $250k original production cost — crude, cheap-looking animation does not cap value; writing and voice do.
- The 6-day cycle is South Park's moat for topicality; a solo AI-assisted creator can be even faster (hours), which is an advantage for trend-jacking — but US-political topicality is local and decays (5.9M → 4.3M → 4.2M).
- For global short-form: copy (1) a primitive, instantly-readable silhouette style; (2) a death/reset running gag like Kenny's — which is structurally perfect for seamless loops (character dies at the end, is back at the start); (3) a few high-contrast archetypes (normal guy / moral guy / monster-ego / unintelligible guy); (4) "equal opportunity offender" posture. Avoid naming real politicians (legal, platform and translation risk).
- Kenny's muffled speech = a character who is funny without intelligible words → transferable across languages (same principle as Bomba's gibberish Sułtan).

### Gaps
- No reliable 2025–2026 figures for South Park's YouTube Shorts channel or global TikTok views by country.
- No current per-episode production budget (only the original $250k).
- S28 premiere exact date not verified (one fan-ranking site suggests Oct 2025; unverified).
- No data on international (non-US) viewership share or dubbed-language performance.

## A5. Cross-series synthesis — what transfers globally vs. what is too local

### Takeaway
Across all four references, what makes them work locally is author voice + vulgar language + topical/national targets; what transfers globally is character architecture, visual running gags, genre parody, sound-based (non-lexical) comedy and production speed. Verbal catchphrases travel only if they are short, in simple English, or tied to a visual/sound cue.

### Cited Findings
- Walaszek's shows build humor on Polish slang, profanity replacing neutral words, and Polish celebrities/politicians — [pl.wikipedia: Blok Ekipa](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blok_Ekipa); Kapitan Bomba satirizes Polish politics and social life — [pl.wikipedia: Kapitan Bomba](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapitan_Bomba)
- Jakub Wędrowycz is read as an embodiment of Polish stereotypes/vices — [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakub_W%C4%99drowycz); but has found popularity in Czech translation — same source
- South Park's 2025 spike came from US political satire (Trump, Noem) — [Straight Arrow News](https://san.com/cc/south-park-premiere-mocking-trump-breaks-26-year-viewership-record/); [Daily Beast](https://www.thedailybeast.com/south-park-breaks-new-record-after-brutal-ice-barbie-kristi-noem-episode)

### Inferences (copy / avoid list for the report writer)
- COPY: one author-voice playing most characters (Walaszek; Parker & Stone) → in ElevenLabs, a small, consistent "voice bible"; micro-episode start (Bomba); fixed release rhythm (Blok Ekipa Tuesdays); flawed-hero archetypes (Bomba: macho failure; Cartman: ego-monster; Wędrowycz: competent degenerate); gibberish/muffled characters (Sułtan, Kenny); death-reset gag (Kenny); genre parody of globally known genres (sci-fi, horror, gangster, corporate); "equal opportunity offender".
- AVOID / ADAPT: wordplay and slang-dependent jokes; national politicians; local celebrities; national stereotypes as the only joke; vulgarity as the whole joke (2026 Bomba revival criticism); long dialogue scenes.
- Catchphrase design for global: ≤4 words, simple English or non-lexical (a scream, a sound, a gesture, a jingle), repeated at the same structural moment each episode (e.g., at the loop point).

## B1. Which humor types travel across languages — research and platform evidence

### Takeaway
Evidence (academic + platform) consistently points to physical/visual comedy, exaggeration, relatable everyday situations and absurd/surreal imagery as the most portable; wordplay, political satire and cultural references are least portable. Platform reports for 2025–2026 add that young audiences' humor is internet-shaped and layered (brainrot, absurdism), that "humor in the lows" and mundane relatability perform on TikTok, and that viewers value visible human effort over AI-generic content.

### Cited Findings
- Commercial translation-agency analysis rates visual/physical comedy as high-portability ("relies on universal physical experience rather than language") and puns/wordplay as low-portability — [Crisol Translations](https://www.crisoltranslations.com/our-blog/translating-humour-across-cultures/) (commercial, not peer-reviewed)
- Caveat that slapstick is not automatically universal: Hong Kong's "mo lei tau" slapstick is considered by some unique/untranslatable because it depends on cultural subtleties — [Wikipedia: Mo lei tau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mo_lei_tau)
- Audiovisual humor translation literature overview (Chiaro-type framing of verbal vs. visual humor in AV media) — [ResearchGate: Translating humour in audiovisual media](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/301246273_Translating_humour_in_audiovisual_media) (not read in full)
- Cross-cultural meme transcreation research (2026) notes humor adaptation requires reconceptualizing cultural scenarios and that findings for one culture pair (China–US) shouldn't be generalized — [arXiv 2602.02510](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.02510)
- YouTube Culture & Trends 2025 ("Creative Maximalism"): 58% of 14–24-year-olds say the internet shaped their sense of humor; internet-referential humor = layers of online inside jokes; Italian brainrot (absurdist AI-meme universe) had 450k+ uploads in 2025; viewers increasingly put a premium on substantial human effort in an era of AI-accelerated production — [YouTube trends PDF](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/trends-25-nextgen.pdf); secondary coverage [Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/design/youtubes-creative-trend-report-is-making-me-feel-old), [Quasa](https://quasa.io/media/youtube-s-2025-culture-trends-report-creators-go-global-irl-and-unfiltered) (figures seen via secondary coverage; verify against PDF)
- YouTube 2025 year recap: **non-verbal Shorts are rising in India — creators use gestures and music instead of voice**; MrBeast gained nearly 50M subscribers in India in 2025, credited to making content accessible in many languages; "brainrot" a defining word of 2025 — [Tubefilter, 15 Dec 2025](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/12/15/youtube-2025-culture-and-trends/)
- TikTok Next 2026 (Jan 2026): audiences want content that "finds humor, community, and connection in the lows"; brands encouraged to include humor and imperfection; signals "Reali-TEA", "Curiosity Detours", "Emotional ROI"; warns that humanizing goes beyond "an AI avatar or chat bot" — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/introducing-tiktok-next-2026-our-trend-forecast-for-marketers-for-the-year-ahead?lang=en); full report [PDF](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/TikTok_Next_2026_Trend_Report.pdf)
- Secondary (unverified) claim: TikTok Creative Center data show character- and situation-based comedy formats growing faster than purely music-driven trends in 2026; absurd humor and exaggerated characters "not a passing fad" — [que.es, 30 Jul 2026](https://www.que.es/2026/07/30/tendencias-tiktok-julio-2026-2/)
- YouTube Animation Trends Report (9 Apr 2026, US survey Apr 2025): 61% of 14–24-year-old animation fans like indie YouTube animated series as much as or more than major-studio series; 63% watch YouTube-made animated series weekly+; **50% of online animation fans aged 14–49 watch animated series in languages other than their own** — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/culture-and-trends/independent-animation-trends-report/)

### Inferences
- Portability ranking for the creator's joke design (high → low): physical slapstick/body humor and misfortune → escalation and reversal (visual payoff) → absurd/surreal imagery (brainrot-adjacent) → universal situations (work, boss, bureaucracy, dating, landlord, gym, phone addiction, AI) → character-based irony → satire of global institutions (big tech, influencers, corporations) → verbal wit → wordplay/national politics (lowest).
- Since half of animation fans already watch in foreign languages, a strong visual hook + simple English or dubbing is enough; the bigger risk is jokes that require cultural decoding.
- "Human effort premium" + TikTok's anti-AI-avatar framing: AI-made series should foreground a distinct authorial style and recurring characters, not generic AI aesthetics.
- Absurd escalation compresses well into 60–120 s and naturally ends in a "reset" — compatible with loop design.

### Gaps
- A 2024 study (Chen & Chang, Chinese vs. Western comedy films) reportedly found slapstick/exaggeration cross cultures while wordplay/political satire/cultural references don't — seen only as a search summary, URL not verified; not cited above as primary evidence.
- A reported finding that ~90% of wordplay excerpts in French dubbing of English TV were translated wordplay-to-wordplay — source URL not verified.
- No peer-reviewed study found specifically on cross-cultural humor in short vertical video.

## B2. Nonverbal / minimal-dialogue animation vs. dialogue-heavy shows — global evidence

### Takeaway
The largest global animated comedy brands on YouTube are nonverbal or near-nonverbal (Oggy, Mr Bean, Larva, Pencilmation), and at least one (Larva) was explicitly designed without dialogue to sell abroad, starting with episodes of "a couple of minutes". Dialogue-heavy shows can globalize too, but only with dubbing/multi-language audio — YouTube reports >25% of watch time from non-primary languages for creators who add dubbed tracks.

### Cited Findings
- **Oggy and the Cockroaches (Xilam):** described as a "non-dialogue comedy franchise"; 32M YouTube subscribers, 17B views, ~5M views/day across 6 localized channels; 20M social followers (Facebook, TikTok, Instagram, Snap); available in 190+ territories; 60M audience in India; particular growth in Africa and Asia; Season 9 greenlit (78 × 7 min) — [Xilam press release via Yahoo Finance, 10 Mar 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/xilam-animation-greenlights-landmark-ninth-164500860.html) (company figures); earlier 600M households in 190 countries — [Xilam 2019](https://xilam.com/en/2019/12/18/oggy-grevin-paris/)
- **Larva (TUBAn, Korea):** director Maeng Joo-gong: made without dialogue to sell overseas because "gestures and facial expressions are a universal language to make viewers laugh"; CEO: "We were thinking about adult viewers from the start"; episodes "only a couple of minutes long"; an episode only airs after all creative staff approve it, weak ones are shelved — [Korea Times, 7 Mar 2013](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/entertainment/shows-dramas/20130307/larva-embraces-all-ages); ~2B YouTube views and 120+ countries by 2019, "limited to no dialogue… universal in its appeal" — [Licensing International](https://licensinginternational.org/news/the-wildflower-group-signs-north-american-representation-agreement-with-tuban-studio-for-larva-series/); later claims of 180+ countries and 11B cumulative views — [Baidu Baike](https://baike.baidu.com/en/item/Larva/1486419) (weak source)
- **Mr Bean (Banijay; live-action + animated):** official channel passed 35M subscribers (Aug 2025); ~75M subscribers across 19 Mr Bean YouTube channels; ~250M across social platforms — [Señal News, 13 Aug 2025](https://senalnews.com/en/digital/banijays-mr-bean-hits-35-million-youtube-subscribers-in-time-for-its-35-years-on-air); 2018: >4.5B views in one year across 200+ countries — [Chortle](https://www.chortle.co.uk/news/2018/10/29/41557/mr_bean_hits_10million_youtube_subscribers)
- **Pencilmation** (nonverbal pencil-drawn gags): ~20M subscribers, ~11.2B views; top video "Pencilmate Needs A Bathroom!" ~269M views (bodily-humor premise) — [Animaker list](https://www.animaker.com/hub/animation-youtube-channels/) (aggregator)
- **Cyanide & Happiness** (adult, dark, stick-figure, short, mostly light dialogue): ~11M subscribers, 3.7B views per Animaker; older figure 3.6M subs — [Animaker](https://www.animaker.com/hub/animation-youtube-channels/); [Wikipedia: The Cyanide & Happiness Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cyanide_%26_Happiness_Show) (figures conflict by date)
- **Dubbing for verbal shows:** YouTube multi-language audio rolled out to all creators (Sept 2025) after a 2-year pilot; creators who uploaded multi-language tracks saw on average >25% of watch time from views in non-primary languages (vs. >15% reported in Feb 2023) — [TechCrunch, 10 Sep 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/10/youtubes-multi-language-audio-feature-for-dubbing-videos-rolls-out-to-all-creators/); [9to5Google 2023](https://9to5google.com/2023/02/23/youtube-multi-language-audio/)
- Non-verbal Shorts rising in India (gestures + music instead of voice) — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/12/15/youtube-2025-culture-and-trends/)
- 50% of animation fans watch in languages other than their own — [YouTube Blog, Apr 2026](https://blog.youtube/culture-and-trends/independent-animation-trends-report/)

### Inferences
- Strategy options for the creator: (1) **Nonverbal core** (gibberish/grunts/SFX/music, Oggy/Larva model) = one master file for all markets, adult tone carried by visuals (violence, gross-out, sexual innuendo, dark endings — Happy Tree Friends-style, inference); (2) **Light-dialogue English + multi-language audio via ElevenLabs dubbing** = South Park-like satire with >25% non-primary-language watch-time upside on YouTube; (3) hybrid: nonverbal main gag + a single on-screen word/caption punchline.
- Oggy's 6 localized channels and growth in Africa/Asia/India suggest that the biggest growth markets for cheap animated comedy are outside the US/EU — tune humor away from Western-only references.
- Larva's internal "shelve weak episodes" rule is a quality gate worth copying when AI makes production cheap.
- Note: most nonverbal mega-brands are kids/family; an adult nonverbal series has fewer direct comps (gap), which is both an opportunity and an uncertainty.

## B3. Profanity/vulgarity across languages and platform rules

### Takeaway
Swearing translates imperfectly: subtitlers tend to tone it down, though a reception study found subtitled swearing is not perceived as stronger than dubbed swearing. On YouTube, since July 2025 strong profanity in the first 7 seconds no longer kills ad revenue, but profanity in titles/thumbnails and high-frequency swearing still limits monetization — so vulgarity should be dosed and kept out of metadata; bleeps can be both a compliance tool and a language-neutral gag.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube (29 July 2025): videos with strong profanity in the first 7 seconds now eligible for full ad revenue; previously demonetized videos may be restored — [Tubefilter, 30 Jul 2025](https://tubefilter.com/2025/07/30/youtube-swearing-first-seven-seconds-policy); still restricted: profanity in titles and thumbnails, and high-frequency usage remains a violation of advertiser-friendly guidelines — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-removes-strong-profanity-restrictions-from-first-7-seconds/); official guideline page (with further updates Feb/Mar 2026 not reviewed) — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9725604?hl=en)
- Reception study: participants did not rate swearwords in subtitles higher than in dubbed clips; the convention of toning down swearing more in subtitles "seems to be based on an invalid assumption" — [Intralinea: Swearwords in Subtitles](https://www.intralinea.org/specials/article/Swearwords_in_Subtitles)
- Descriptive studies show frequent toning down or omission of swearing in subtitles in several language pairs (e.g., Arabic–English for ideological/cultural reasons) — [UKM record: Arabic–English swearword subtitling](https://libmast.utm.my/Record/ukm.eprints-18564/Description); further case studies — [De Gruyter (Intercultural Pragmatics 2019)](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/ip-2019-0021/pdf) (not read in full)
- Kapitan Bomba faced strong criticism from "moral guardians" for its volume of profanity (snippet) — [All The Tropes](https://allthetropes.org/wiki/Kapitan_Bomba) (unverified, page 403)

### Inferences
- Polish "kurwa"-style profanity carries rhythm and meaning that do not map 1:1 to English "fuck"; when dubbing via ElevenLabs, write separate swear lines per language rather than literal translation.
- Use a **bleep as a running gag** (universal sound, works in every language, keeps titles/thumbnails clean, reduces high-frequency-profanity flags).
- Put the vulgarity in situations and visuals (gross-out, dark outcomes) rather than in dense swearing — this survives translation and platform moderation better.
- Hook rule update: a swear in the first seconds is now monetization-safe on YouTube; still keep titles/thumbnails clean.

### Gaps
- No verified, current TikTok/Instagram policy text on profanity reach (For You eligibility) retrieved.
- No data found on how adult animated channels (e.g., Cyanide & Happiness, South Park) handle multiple-language releases of profane content.
